Every engineer on the team shares responsibility for handling incidents well. A clear, repeatable process keeps customer impact short, communication transparent, and root causes understood. This runbook walks you through the full incident lifecycle — from the moment something breaks to the post-incident review that prevents it from happening again.

## Severity Levels

Use the table below to classify an incident as soon as you declare it. When in doubt, err on the side of the higher severity — you can always downgrade once you have more information.

| Severity | Label | Definition | Example |
|----------|-------|------------|---------|
| **P1** | Critical | Production is down or data loss is occurring | Checkout flow returning 500s; database writes failing |
| **P2** | Major | A major feature is degraded; no data loss | Search returning stale results; slow API responses (>5s) |
| **P3** | Minor | A non-critical feature is broken; no data loss | Dashboard widget not rendering; a low-traffic endpoint erroring |
| **P4** | Cosmetic | UI or copy issue; no functional impact | Wrong colour on a button; typo on a marketing page |

## Roles

Every P1 and P2 incident requires three roles to be filled before triage begins. Assign them explicitly in the war room channel.

- **Incident Commander (IC)** — Owns the incident end-to-end. Keeps the team focused, drives decisions, and ensures the communication cadence is met. The IC does _not_ fix things directly.
- **Tech Lead** — Investigates the root cause, proposes and implements the fix or rollback, and coordinates any required infrastructure changes.
- **Comms Lead** — Updates stakeholders and the incident channel on schedule. Drafts the status-page message and any customer-facing communication.

For P3 and P4 incidents a single engineer can cover all three roles.

## Response Procedure

<Steps>
  <Step title="Declare the incident">
    Run the slash command in the `#incidents` Slack channel. Replace `p1` with the appropriate severity.

    ```
    /incident declare p1 Checkout flow returning 500s for all users
    ```

    This command automatically creates a dedicated war room channel (e.g. `#incident-2024-06-15-checkout`) and opens a linked PagerDuty incident. Join that channel immediately and pin the PagerDuty link.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Assign roles">
    Post a role assignment message in the war room channel as the first thing you do. Use this template:

    ```
    🚨 Roles assigned
    IC: @alice
    Tech Lead: @bob
    Comms Lead: @carol
    ```

    If you cannot fill a role, ping `@on-call-lead` immediately.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Identify scope">
    Before forming any hypothesis, establish the blast radius. Answer these questions in the war room channel:

    - Which services, endpoints, or features are affected?
    - How many users are impacted (approximate percentage or absolute number)?
    - When did the issue begin? Check Datadog dashboards and deployment history.
    - Is the issue isolated to a single region or environment?
  </Step>

  <Step title="Assess impact and form a hypothesis">
    With scope established, the Tech Lead proposes the most likely root cause in a short message prefixed with `🔍 Hypothesis:`. Align on the hypothesis before touching anything in production. Common starting points:

    - Did a deployment happen within the last 30 minutes? Check ArgoCD.
    - Did a config change or feature flag change go out?
    - Are any upstream dependencies (databases, third-party APIs) reporting issues?
  </Step>

  <Step title="Implement fix or rollback">
    Execute the agreed remediation. If you cannot identify a fix within 20 minutes, roll back to the last known good deployment:

    ```bash
    # Roll back via ArgoCD CLI
    argocd app rollback <app-name> --revision <previous-revision>

    # Or via kubectl
    kubectl rollout undo deployment/<deployment-name> -n <namespace>
    ```

    Document every action taken in the war room channel with a timestamp so the timeline is accurate for the PIR.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Communicate on schedule">
    The Comms Lead posts a status update on this cadence, even if there is nothing new to report:

    - **P1** — Every 15 minutes
    - **P2** — Every 30 minutes

    Use this template:

    ```
    ⏱ [14:45 UTC] Update #3
    Status: Investigating
    Impact: ~15% of users cannot complete checkout
    Next update: 15:00 UTC
    ```

    Update the public status page at `https://status.<your-domain>` for any P1 or customer-visible P2.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Resolve the incident">
    Once the service is fully restored and metrics confirm recovery (error rate back to baseline, latency normal), run the resolve command in the war room channel:

    ```
    /incident resolve
    ```

    Post a final summary in the war room channel covering: what broke, what fixed it, and the total duration. Then send a brief all-clear message in `#incidents`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Schedule the post-incident review">
    Open a PIR ticket in Jira immediately after resolution — do not wait until you feel ready to write it. Assign it to the Incident Commander and set the due date to 5 business days from the incident date. The IC is responsible for scheduling the 60-minute PIR meeting and circulating a draft document beforehand.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Never silently resolve an incident by just fixing the underlying issue without running `/incident resolve` in Slack and posting a summary. Silent resolutions leave stakeholders without confirmation, corrupt your incident metrics, and mean no PIR is triggered. Every incident must be formally closed, no matter how small.
</Warning>

## Post-Incident Review (PIR)

A PIR is **required for all P1 and P2 incidents** and must be completed within 5 business days. The goal is blameless learning, not attribution. The PIR document should cover:

- **Timeline** — A minute-by-minute account of what happened, with timestamps.
- **Root cause** — The underlying technical cause (not "human error").
- **Contributing factors** — Everything that made the incident worse or harder to detect.
- **What went well** — Steps the team took that shortened the incident.
- **Action items** — Concrete, assigned, time-boxed tasks to prevent recurrence.

Post the completed PIR document in the `#incidents` channel and link it to the PagerDuty incident record.

<Note>
  P3 and P4 incidents do not require a formal PIR, but you are encouraged to add a brief retrospective comment to the associated Jira ticket if the issue exposed a gap in monitoring or documentation.
</Note>
